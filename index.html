Why DAN needs a pathway-first search

DAN students can credibly pursue finance, consulting, analytics, technology, marketing analytics, healthcare, sports, and public-impact roles. That flexibility is powerful, but it requires a clear story: the problem type you want to solve, the data or model skills you can use, and the business decision you can influence.

Two searches, one evidence strategy

Undergraduate students should treat the internship search as the first proof point and the full-time search as the conversion strategy. The strongest senior-year stories connect coursework, projects, internships, dashboards, models, and stakeholder communication into one coherent DAN value proposition.

Timing tracks for the DAN search
DAN students should use the same evidence-building logic for both searches: prove quantitative rigor, business translation, and decision impact.
Search 1 · Summer internship
Secure a first proof point: model, analysis, dashboard, business recommendation, finance/analytics project, or technical documentation that anchors senior-year recruiting.
Search 2 · Full-time job
Convert DAN coursework, internship evidence, quantitative projects, and alumni/employer outreach into a focused offer strategy.
Summer internship search. The internship should create a concrete analytics artifact. Search by pathway + title + employer type + geography + evidence you can produce; do not search broadly for “analytics jobs.”
Goal: choose two role-family hypotheses and search terms that match DAN evidence.
  • Pick one primary pathway and one backup. Choose from finance/risk, consulting, data/statistical modeling, tech/operations, marketing/growth, or domain analytics.
  • Select 3-4 searchable titles. Start with Financial Analyst, Business Analyst, Data Analyst, Digital Marketing Analyst, Investment Analyst, Programmer Analyst, or Sales & Trading Analyst variants.
  • Translate current courses into evidence. Use STA 112, BEM 251, FIN 231, MTH 121, MTH 253, STA 363, and BEM 392 language to show quantitative and business readiness.
Goal: apply to roles where you can talk about data, models, metrics, and decisions.
  • Build a 40-employer list. Include DAN historical employers, finance firms, consulting firms, sports/healthcare/public-impact organizations, product/operations teams, and analytics-forward employers.
  • Submit 8-12 targeted applications weekly while roles are open. Tailor bullets around modeling, statistics, dashboards, operations research, finance, and business translation.
  • Pair priority applications with outreach. Ask alumni and recent graduates about first projects, tools used, data sources, team structure, and intern success factors.
Goal: explain methods, assumptions, tradeoffs, and business implications clearly.
  • Prepare one quantitative project story. Explain the problem, data, method, assumption, result, limitation, and recommended decision.
  • Practice explaining models to nontechnical managers. Employers value the DAN student who can connect math to business decisions.
  • Expect mixed interviews. Prepare for behavioral questions, Excel/case prompts, data interpretation, business scenarios, and technical explanations.

Weekly internship-search rhythm

  • 8-12 applications to roles aligned with one of your two DAN pathways.
  • 5-7 outreach messages to alumni, analysts, internship contacts, or recruiters.
  • One technical proof update: model, chart, dashboard, code sample, memo, or project summary.
  • One interview rep explaining a quantitative project in business language.

Internship evidence targets

  • One model or quantitative analysis.
  • One dashboard or visualization artifact.
  • One decision memo or slide story.
  • One story connecting data to business or stakeholder action.
Full-time job search. Your full-time search should convert DAN projects and internship evidence into proof that you can help an organization make better decisions with data, models, and business judgment.
Goal: enter senior recruiting with a coherent quantitative + business story.
  • Convert internship and class projects into portfolio proof. Build one model story, one visualization story, and one business recommendation story.
  • Choose one primary and one adjacent role family. Use DAN job title evidence and employer cards to define your search terms.
  • Update resume and LinkedIn with tool + decision language. Include statistics, modeling, operations research, finance, visualization, and business translation where relevant.
Goal: build sufficient pipeline before deadlines close.
  • Submit 6-10 targeted applications weekly. Prioritize roles where your DAN proof maps directly to job requirements.
  • Build a 50-employer target list. Blend historical DAN employers, analytics-forward firms, finance/consulting targets, regional employers, and growth companies.
  • Network into teams, not just companies. Ask alumni about data teams, analytics tools, business problems, new-grad programs, and hiring timelines.
Goal: adjust the pipeline based on evidence, not anxiety.
  • If you are not getting interviews: tighten titles, add keywords, show clearer project evidence, and increase referral volume.
  • If interviews are not converting: practice explaining assumptions, tradeoffs, and business impact with a coach.
  • If a target market is saturated: expand to adjacent employers that need decision support, forecasting, optimization, finance analytics, customer analytics, or business intelligence.

Weekly full-time-search rhythm

  • 6-10 targeted applications tied to one primary or adjacent role family.
  • 6-8 alumni/employer outreach messages, prioritizing teams with analytics or finance roles.
  • One technical interview practice rep and one behavioral/story practice rep.
  • Friday pipeline review: applications, contacts, interviews, follow-ups, and conversion rate.

DAN search assets

  • Role-specific resume with quantitative methods and business impact.
  • Portfolio artifact: model, dashboard, code sample, or decision memo.
  • LinkedIn profile naming Decision Analytics, tools, domains, projects, and target roles.
  • Clear answer to: what decision did your analysis improve?

I am targeting [pathway] roles where I can use [priority skill] to help teams make better decisions.         Interview proof points

        Prepare three stories: one technical analysis story, one business communication story, and one teamwork or ambiguity story. Each should show method, judgment, and impact.
